## The 4 Golden Pillars of Design (Robin Williams's CRAP)

Every graphic, poster, banner, slide, document, or screen MUST strictly obey these 4 laws:

### 1. Contrast (Контраст)
- **The Core Rule**: If two items are not exactly the same, make them **DRASTICALLY DIFFERENT**.
- Never create "almost identical" differences (e.g., using a 14pt subtitle next to a 15pt heading, or dark gray on black).
- Use contrast through:
  - **Size**: Massive headline vs. delicate body copy.
  - **Weight**: Heavy bold sans-serif vs. thin regular.
  - **Color**: High-chroma warm accent vs. cool muted background.
  - **Shape & Texture**: Organic illustration against crisp geometric cards.

### 2. Repetition (Повторение)
- **The Core Rule**: Repeat visual elements throughout the entire piece to create unity and brand recognition.
- Repeat consistent bullet shapes, color accents, typography families, border radiuses, and spatial intervals.
- Repetition turns random elements into a cohesive, professional identity.

### 3. Alignment (Выравнивание)
- **The Core Rule**: **NOTHING** should be placed on a page arbitrarily. Every element must have a visual connection to something else on the page.
- Choose **one strong alignment edge** (usually left-aligned).
- **Avoid centered text** for long paragraphs — centered text creates soft, jagged edges on both sides that are exhausting to read. Use centering only for short, formal headlines or badges.

### 4. Proximity (Приближение)
- **The Core Rule**: Group related items close together; separate unrelated items with generous negative space.
- An eye should perceive 3 to 5 logical clusters on a page, not 15 scattered pieces.
- Never let distance between a heading and its paragraph equal the distance to the preceding block.

## Grid Architecture & Spatial Layout

### 1. The Anatomy of a Grid
- **Margins**: The outer breathing space framing the canvas. Wider margins create luxury, focus, and elegance.
- **Columns**: Vertical guidelines for content. 12-column grids offer maximum flexibility (divisible by 2, 3, 4, 6).
- **Gutters**: The negative space between columns (standard: 16px, 24px, 32px). Never place content inside a gutter.
- **Baseline Grid**: Horizontal lines (typically 4px or 8px) to which all typography baselines lock, establishing vertical rhythm.

### 2. Compositional Rules (Golden Ratio & Rule of Thirds)
- **The Rule of Thirds**: Divide the frame with 2 horizontal and 2 vertical lines. Place focal subjects on the 4 intersection points.
- **Golden Spiral / Fibonacci**: Guide the user's viewing path from the expansive periphery inward toward the focal message.
- **Negative Space (White Space)**: White space is not empty space; it is a primary design element that directs attention and conveys premium quality.

## Modern Geometric Logo & Identity Principles (Logo Modernism)
When ideating or evaluating brand marks:
1. **Geometric Simplicity**: Pure primary shapes (circle, square, equilateral triangle) create timeless, instantly recognizable emblems.
2. **Negative Space**: Utilize Gestalt figure-ground principles where the space *between* objects creates a secondary meaningful symbol.
3. **Scalability & Silhouettes**: A logo must work in pure monochrome (100% black on white) and remain legible at 16x16 pixels as a favicon.

## Practical Design Review Checklist
Before approving any visual work, run this quick diagnostic:
- [ ] **Contrast**: Is the most important element immediately obvious in a 1-second squint test?
- [ ] **Repetition**: Are colors and typefaces limited to a strict palette of 2–3 values?
- [ ] **Alignment**: Does every element snap to a common invisible vertical or horizontal axis?
- [ ] **Proximity**: Are related labels, titles, and descriptions grouped without ambiguous gaps?
- [ ] **Breathing Room**: Is there sufficient negative space around the edges?
