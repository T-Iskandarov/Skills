Before you start designing user interfaces, it's crucial to know what actually makes them good. Don't treat this part as a definitive checklist - more as a beginner's guide to creating good user interfaces. The topic of Ul Design is vast, and it can't be broken down to "a few simple steps." What are grids in Ul Design? As defined on the previous page, a grid is a set of horizontal and vertical lines, which divide the screen into columns and rows. Grids give structure to a page / an app and ensure consistent spacing between elements. First, let's take a look at the anatomy. Columns Columns are the vertical sections of a grid. The more columns the grid has, the more flexible it is. Most web designs use 12 columns. Rows Rows are horizontal sections of a grid. They're used relatively rarely in web design. Most usually, grids are based on columns. GRIDS 38

Gutters Gutters are empty spaces that divide columns and rows. The smaller the gutter, the tighter the content on the screen. Margins Margins are the space outside of columns/rows and gutters. You can think of them as gutters outside of the grid. While they can be set to O, setting them to a higher value will result in more white space on the edges of your design. It's vital to get margins right. Using margins that are too low can make your design look worse because of less white space. GRIDS 39
